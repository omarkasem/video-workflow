## Read first
Read README.md, project/project.json, and project/state.json. Read the relevant guide before its stage. The sources/legacy/ Markdown is archival, NOT approved direction. Do not adopt its timestamps or pricing.

## The owner's priorities
The song and two supplied character designs already exist. Do not replace them. The owner wants to SEE and approve the WHOLE film cheaply before high-quality images or paid video. Use a mixture of still-image editorial motion, a few generated hero motions, and approved reuse. Keep Kling and Google's video routes interchangeable where their verified capabilities permit.

## Non-negotiable gates
1. Timing and visual-world draft -> owner review.
2. Rough multi-panel storyboard sheets covering the full audio -> owner approval.
3. Full-length rough animatic with the actual song -> owner approval.
4. Individual high-quality frames/contact sheet -> owner approval.
5. Provider capability test, itemized cost and shot list -> explicit production authorization.
6. Small CapCut pilot -> full assembly -> export review.

No approval may be inferred from silence. Approval must name a stage/version or specific shot IDs. Storyboard approval is not permission to spend unlimited credits, change accounts, buy plans, or publish. Before video generation, both the animatic and relevant high-quality frames must be approved. A capability test that consumes credits also needs authorization.

## Work and resume safely
Use stable shot IDs and versioned filenames. Record decisions, output paths, job IDs, actual costs, and the next action in project/state.json or referenced per-shot records. Check completed assets and pending jobs before generating anything. A timeout is not a reason to submit a second paid job. Never regenerate approved, unchanged work. Changing a shot marks dependent frames/clips as stale; it does not invalidate unrelated scenes.

Do not write passwords, cookies, or API secrets into these files. Use existing authorized interfaces. A Markdown file does not provide missing tools or permissions. Check local/browser/computer-use capability instead of claiming it exists. Respect service restrictions; do not bypass a refusal or silently switch to an unapproved paid route.

## Technical invariants
Use the original song as the master soundtrack and preserve its musical tail. The normalized SRT is a formatting draft, not verified alignment. Do not use automatic captions as the lyric source of truth. The production timeline must cover the full audio with no unexplained gaps/overlaps and use integer frame boundaries after the frame rate is approved.

Never feed a complete storyboard grid to image-to-video as one scene. Never treat an overview sheet as final-quality footage. Make clean individual frames from approved compositions and original references. An end frame is optional unless a particular shot needs it; unrelated first/last images are not a substitute for an editorial cut.
